# Brandmaster — Promotional Script

**Format:** Presentation or narrated product vision  
**Estimated length:** 4–5 minutes  
**Audience:** Business leaders, teams who work with brand data, and potential partner teams

---

## Opening: The everyday problem

**[Visual: A brand name appears in several spreadsheets and applications, with different spellings and IDs.]**

**Narrator:**

A brand shows up in a new file.

Is it a brand we already know? Is it a new one? Is it the same brand under a different name? Which record should the company use?

Today, answering those questions can mean searching through tables, comparing spreadsheets, asking colleagues, and making a decision that another team may have to make all over again.

When every team or application keeps its own answer, brand information becomes inconsistent. Duplicate records grow. Reports become harder to trust. And simple work takes longer than it should.

**Meet Brandmaster: one place to recognize, review, and improve brand information.**

---

## How Brandmaster works today

**[Visual: A user opens Brandmaster and adds a list of unmapped brands.]**

**Narrator:**

Using Brandmaster starts with the brands that need attention.

A team member can bring in a list of unfamiliar or unmapped brands. Brandmaster checks each one against the information available to the team, including known brands, aliases, and previous decisions.

**[Visual: Suggested matches appear, with a few items marked for review.]**

For a likely match, Brandmaster shows a recommendation. When the answer is less certain, it leaves the decision for a person to review.

The reviewer can confirm the match, correct the name or identifier, add a note, or flag the record for more investigation. This keeps people in control while helping them avoid repeating checks that have already been done.

**[Visual: Reviewed items move to a finished list, then a clean mapping file is downloaded.]**

When the review is complete, the team can prepare a consistent mapping file for the existing upload process. The team can also retain decisions and track work, so future reviews have useful history to build on.

---

## A real-world use case

**[Visual: A catalog team receives a batch of supplier product listings.]**

**Narrator:**

Imagine a catalog team receives hundreds of listings from different suppliers.

One supplier writes “North Star,” another writes “NorthStar,” and a third uses a brand name the company has never seen. Without a shared process, the team may create duplicate records or spend hours checking each one by hand.

With Brandmaster, the team brings the list into one review process. Known names and aliases can point to existing records. Possible duplicates are surfaced. New or uncertain brands are sent to a person to decide.

The team spends its attention where judgment is needed—and keeps approved answers for next time.

That means less repeated research, cleaner brand information, and more confidence in the catalog and reports that depend on it.

---

## The future: from mapping tool to brand quality hub

**[Visual: Brandmaster compares a “Known Brands” table with an “Unknown Brands” list.]**

**Narrator:**

The bigger opportunity is to grow Brandmaster beyond preparing mapping files.

In the future, Brandmaster could connect directly to the company’s established Brand table and Unknown Brand table. It could compare what is already known with what is new, spot incomplete or conflicting records, and help teams decide what should change.

Teams could use Brandmaster to standardize names, reconcile aliases, identify duplicates, and merge records that represent the same brand. AI and matching rules could find likely connections and explain why an item needs attention. People would review important or uncertain changes before they are approved.

Approved improvements could then be returned to the company’s main Brand table, with a clear history of what changed and who approved it.

Brandmaster would not just help clean up a batch once. It could help the company keep its brand information useful and trustworthy over time.

---

## The future: a shared service for other applications

**[Visual: Several company applications connect to Brandmaster.]**

**Narrator:**

And what if every application did not have to solve brand identity on its own?

A product entry form could ask Brandmaster, “Have we seen this brand before?”

A catalog tool could ask, “Which approved brand record should I use?”

A reporting system could check, “Is this brand information valid and current?”

Brandmaster could return a clear answer: a confirmed match, a possible match that needs review, or a brand that is not yet known. If a person needs to make the call, the question could go into the Brandmaster review queue.

That gives dependent applications one trusted place to check—rather than separate lists and rules that drift apart.

---

## Advancement plan

**[Visual: A simple five-step path appears.]**

**Narrator:**

We can build toward this vision in practical steps.

**First: strengthen the current workflow.** Make recommendations easier to understand, improve team visibility, and keep decisions clear and reviewable.

**Next: connect shared brand information.** Bring the established Brand table and Unknown Brand table into the work so teams can compare records in one place.

**Then: improve the source records.** Add controlled editing, standardization, alias management, and duplicate merging, with human approval and a clear history before changes are written back.

**After that: let applications check brands.** Give approved company applications a consistent way to ask whether a brand is known, which record it belongs to, and whether it needs review.

**Finally: grow with real demand.** Add useful data sources, integrations, quality reporting, and broader coverage as teams adopt the service.

Each step delivers practical value on its own and moves Brandmaster toward a shared company capability.

---

## Closing: The value of Brandmaster

**[Visual: A clean, shared brand record appears across multiple applications.]**

**Narrator:**

Brand information may seem like a small detail—until different answers slow down teams, multiply records, and undermine confidence in company data.

Brandmaster gives the company a way to bring those answers together: use existing knowledge, let AI help find patterns, involve people where judgment matters, and make approved decisions reusable.

Today, it helps teams review and prepare brand mappings. Tomorrow, it can help maintain trusted brand records and give every dependent application a consistent answer.

**Brandmaster: one trusted view of every brand, ready for every team and every system that depends on it.**

---

## Short version (30 seconds)

Brand names arrive in different forms, and teams often repeat the same research to decide whether a brand is new, already known, or a duplicate. Brandmaster brings that work into one place. Today, teams can check likely matches, review uncertain cases, and prepare clean mapping files. Over time, Brandmaster can help improve the company’s main brand records and let other applications check brand identity and validity using the same trusted information. AI helps find patterns; people guide the decisions that matter. **One trusted view of every brand, shared across the company.**

## Presenter note

Describe direct Brand-table updates and application-wide brand checks as future capabilities unless they have been implemented and approved for the specific deployment. The current product supports brand review, reference-based recommendations, team workflows, and mapping-file preparation; available features can vary by deployment.
